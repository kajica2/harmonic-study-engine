## 19th-Century Harmonic Expansion

### Beethoven
- **Harmonic Innovation:** Remote modulations; mediant relationships; Neapolitan chords; augmented sixths; diminished sevenths; delayed resolution; dramatic silence.
- **Key Works:** Eroica; Fifth Symphony; Ninth Symphony; late string quartets; Hammerklavier.

- **Harmonic Innovation:** Programmatic harmony; idée fixe; chromatic diminished chords; unusual resolutions; orchestral color as harmonic force.
- **Key Works:** Symphonie fantastique; Harold in Italy; Roméo et Juliette.

- **Study Prompt:** Map the idée fixe transformations in *Symphonie fantastique* and note how harmony changes each time.

- **Study Prompt:** Analyze the Preludes for non-functional chords, pedal points, and modal borrowing.

- **Harmonic Innovation:** Thematic transformation; augmented triads; tritones; unresolved dissonance; extreme chromaticism.
- **Key Works:** B minor Sonata; Faust Symphony; Nuages gris.

### Wagner
- **Harmonic Innovation:** Tristan chord; chromatic delay; leitmotif; tonal ambiguity; endless melody; unresolved longing.
- **Key Works:** Tristan und Isolde; Parsifal; Der Ring des Nibelungen.

**Tristan Chord**
- Spelling: F – B – D# – G#
- Intervals: augmented fourth, major third, perfect fifth (stacked)
- Function: Usually analyzed as an altered bVI or French sixth; its resolution is ambiguous and constantly deferred.
- Resolution targets: E7 (V7), Am (i), G7, F#m, nothing — deferred.

- **Notes:** Wagner never lets the Tristan chord settle. Each resolution is immediately re-dissonated, so the harmony behaves like endless longing. This is the practical end of functional tonality in the 19th century.
- **One-line Takeaway:** Made chromatic delay and tonal ambiguity central to musical drama.
- **Study Prompt:** Reduce the opening of *Tristan* to intervals and Roman numerals; identify what never resolves.

## Modern Classical

### Debussy
- **Harmonic Innovation:** Non-functional harmony; parallelism; whole-tone and pentatonic scales; modal color; extended chords as sonority.
- **Key Works:** Prélude à l'après-midi d'un faune; La Mer; Préludes; Voiles.

**Chord Changes / Collections — Voiles**

| Bars | Chord / Collection | Roman |
|------|-------------------|-------|
| 1–10 | Whole-tone on C (C–D–E–F#–G#–A#) | No function |
| 11–20 | Whole-tone continued; parallel augmented triads | No function |
| 21–30 | Pentatonic on C (C–D–E–G–A) | Modal color |
| 31–40 | Return of whole-tone collection | No function |
| 41–48 | Whole-tone with C pedal; parallel chords over drone | Static |

**Chord Blocks**
- Caug – Daug – Eaug – F#aug – G#aug – A#aug (parallel augmented triads)
- Pentatonic: C – D – E – G – A (no leading tone)
- Whole-tone: no perfect fifth, no leading tone, no V–I

- **Notes:** Because the whole-tone scale has no perfect fifths, functional harmony is impossible. Debussy replaces progression with color, register, and parallelism. Chords are sonorities, not functions.
- **One-line Takeaway:** Moved harmony from function to color.
- **Study Prompt:** Analyze *Voiles* for whole-tone structure and *La cathédrale engloutie* for parallel chords.

### Stravinsky
- **Harmonic Innovation:** Polytonality and bitonality; ostinato; static harmony; dissonant chord blocks; irregular meter.
- **Key Works:** The Rite of Spring; Petrushka; The Firebird; Symphony of Psalms.

**Petrushka Chord**
- Spelling: C – E – G plus F# – A# – C#
- Intervals: Two major triads a tritone apart
- Effect: Bitonal, unresolved, percussive

- **Notes:** Stravinsky's harmony is block-based and static. Chords do not progress; they repeat, collide, and accumulate dissonance. Rhythm and register do the structural work that harmony did in the 19th century.
- **One-line Takeaway:** Made rhythm and harmonic violence central.
- **Study Prompt:** Analyze the Petrushka chord, C major plus F-sharp major, and the chord blocks in *The Rite of Spring*.

### Schoenberg
- **Harmonic Innovation:** Emancipation of dissonance; atonality; twelve-tone method; rows; hexachords; inversion; retrograde.
- **Key Works:** Verklärte Nacht; Pierrot Lunaire; Erwartung; Suite for Piano; Variations for Orchestra.

**Row Forms — Suite for Piano op. 25**

| Form | Pitches |
|------|---------|
| P0 | E – F – G – Db – Gb – Eb – Ab – D – B – C – A – Bb |
| I0 | E – Eb – Db – G – D – F – C – Ab – Bb – A – C# – B |
| R0 | Bb – A – C – B – D – Ab – Eb – Gb – Db – G – F – E |
| RI0 | B – C# – A – Bb – Ab – C – F – D – G – Db – Eb – E |

**Hexachords**
- First hexachord of P0: E – F – G – Db – Gb – Eb
- Second hexachord of P0: Ab – D – B – C – A – Bb
- Hexachords are combinatorially related; together they cover all twelve pitches.

**Structural Plan**

| Bars | Chord / Event | Roman |
|------|---------------|-------|
| 1–4 | P0 segment: E – F – G – Db | Row form |
| 5–8 | P0 segment: Gb – Eb – Ab – D | Row form |
| 9–12 | I0 statement | Inversion |
| 13–16 | R0 statement | Retrograde |
| 17–20 | RI0 statement | Retrograde inversion |
| 21–24 | P0 and I0 combined | Combinatoriality |

- **Notes:** Harmony is organized by row forms rather than chord function. Consonance and dissonance are no longer opposites. Any vertical sonority is legitimate if the row is preserved.
- **One-line Takeaway:** Organized harmony without keys.
- **Study Prompt:** Build a row matrix for a twelve-tone work and trace inversion, retrograde, and hexachord use.

### Bartók
- **Harmonic Innovation:** Folk modes; pentatonicism; quartal harmony; polychords; symmetrical scales; axis system.
- **Key Works:** Mikrokosmos; Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta; string quartets; Concerto for Orchestra.

**Axis System**
- Tonic axis: A – C – Eb – F#
- Dominant axis: E – G – Bb – C#
- Subdominant axis: D – F – Ab – B
- Principle: Keys relate by tritone and minor third rather than by fifth.

- **Notes:** The fugue subject enters on successive fifths (A, E, B, F#, C#, G#, D#, A#), which is traditional. But Bartók then replaces the dominant relationship with the tritone axis A–Eb, creating a symmetrical, non-functional harmonic world. Quartal chords and folk modes appear throughout.
- **One-line Takeaway:** Fused folk modality with modern symmetry and chromaticism.
- **Study Prompt:** Analyze the axis system in *Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta*.

### Cage
- **Harmonic Innovation:** Silence; noise; chance; duration; prepared piano; indeterminacy.
- **Key Works:** 4′33″; Sonatas and Interludes; Music of Changes.

**Duration Structure — 4′33″**

| Movement | Chord | Roman |
|----------|-------|-------|
| I | Silence | No intentional harmony |
| II | Silence | No intentional harmony |
| III | Silence | No intentional harmony |

**Durations:** 30 seconds; 2 minutes 23 seconds; 1 minute 40 seconds.

**Prepared Piano — Sonatas and Interludes**
- Method: Objects placed on piano strings to alter timbre.
- Harmonic effect: Pitch is present but timbre dominates; modes and gamelan-like sonorities replace functional harmony.

- **Notes:** Cage removes the composer's control over pitch relationships. Harmony becomes ambient sound, duration, and timbre. For a harmonic study page, Cage represents the limit case: harmony is one option among many, and can be abandoned entirely.
- **One-line Takeaway:** Made harmony optional—and sometimes absent.
- **Study Prompt:** Study *4′33″* as the removal of intentional harmony; analyze prepared-piano timbre in *Sonatas and Interludes*.

### Stockhausen
- **Harmonic Innovation:** Total serialism; electronic sound; spatialization; moment form; serialized pitch, duration, dynamics, timbre.
- **Key Works:** Gesang der Jünglinge; Kontakte; Gruppen; Mantra.

**Formula — Mantra**
- Pitches: A – Bb – D – Eb – F – F# – G – B – C – C# – E – G#
- Note: Thirteen-note melodic formula; each parameter (pitch, duration, dynamics, timbre, register) is serialized.

**Structural Plan — Mantra**

| Section | Chord / Event | Roman |
|---------|---------------|-------|
| 1 | Formula in original form | Serialized |
| 2 | Formula with duration series inverted | Serialized |
| 3 | Formula with dynamics reversed | Serialized |
| 4 | Formula with register displaced | Serialized |
| 5 | Formula with timbre altered | Serialized |
| 6 | Formula in retrograde | Serialized |
| 7 | Formula fragmented and layered | Serialized |

**Electronic Works**
- *Gesang der Jünglinge*: Fused boy's voice with electronic sound; spatial placement of sound.
- *Kontakte*: Serialized electronic and instrumental sound; four-channel spatialization.
- *Gruppen*: Three orchestras placed around the audience; spatial harmony.

- **Notes:** Stockhausen serializes every parameter, so pitch harmony is only one dimension of a total organization. Space and timbre become compositional forces alongside chords.
- **One-line Takeaway:** Pushed composition into electronics, space, and total organization.
- **Study Prompt:** Study *Kontakte* or *Gesang der Jünglinge*; ask how pitch, duration, and dynamics are serialized.
